## Naming / Domain

Public product name: `67speed`.

Purchased domain: `67speed.com`.

Roger’s explicit ambition: design for a “10 million visitors in one day” consumer moment, not a niche developer launch.

Why this name works:

- Short enough to remember after hearing the meme once.
- Ties directly to `6-7` while avoiding a trademarked platform or celebrity name.
- “Speed” suggests an instant test, score, challenge, or game rather than a static explainer.
- Works as both a URL and a spoken prompt: “Go test your 67 speed.”
- Broad enough for a meme speed test, reaction generator, share-card maker, daily challenge, leaderboard, and party/classroom toy.

Avoid these risks:

- Do not rely on copyrighted music, TikTok audio, NBA footage, LaMelo Ball likeness, Taylen Kinney likeness, or reposted creator clips.
- Do not present the meme as having one fixed authoritative meaning.
- Do not build a thin SEO spam site full of autogenerated low-value trend pages.
- Do not use “official” unless there is actual authorization.

## Trend Understanding

The `6-7` / `six seven` meme is popular because it is intentionally ambiguous and absurd. It originated from the Skrilla track `Doot Doot (6 7)`, spread through basketball edits, especially because LaMelo Ball is 6'7", and then escaped into a broader Gen Alpha / Gen Z call-and-response.

Core ingredients:

- Phrase: `6-7`, `67`, `6 7`, usually spoken as “six SEVEN”.
- Gesture: raised/lowered hands in a see-saw motion, palms up.
- Meaning: no fixed meaning; it can signal random, mid, awkward, chaotic, funny, or “you had to be there”.
- Social mechanic: people say it when numbers, ratings, height, scores, or awkward moments appear.
- Cultural shape: part meme, part playground password, part brainrot, part shared nonsense.
- Spread pattern: song lyric → basketball edits → creator repetition → kids/schools/group chats → mainstream explainers and backlash.

The product should embrace the nonsense rather than over-explain it.

## Pitch

A viral web speed test for nonsense: open `67speed.com`, answer or tap through a tiny chaotic challenge, and get a shareable `6-7` speed score.

## Why It Matters

Most consumer meme tools are either too slow, locked inside one social platform, or require editing skill. `67speed` should be a zero-friction meme participation machine: no login, no app install, no video editor, no copyrighted clips required.

If the trend is moving at global scale, the winning product is not a deep utility. It is a fast, funny artifact people can send in a group chat, screenshot, remix, and challenge friends with.

The goal is not “explain six seven.” The goal is “give people a reason to send someone to `67speed.com` right now.”

## Qualification

### Pub Test

Can this be explained clearly in one sentence? Yes: “A one-tap speed test that tells you how dangerously `6-7` your reflexes, vibe, and nonsense energy are.”

Why it lands:

- Everyone understands speed tests and silly scores.
- The meme itself has no fixed meaning, so scoring the “vibe” is the joke.
- The domain is memorable and immediately tied to the phrase.
- It is useful to non-developers, kids, parents, teachers, meme accounts, group chats, and creators.

### Competitors / Adjacent Tools

- TikTok templates — strongest distribution surface, but not a standalone destination and often depend on copyrighted audio or platform-specific editing.
- Instagram Reels templates — similar to TikTok; easy sharing but weak canonical destination.
- CapCut meme templates — powerful for edits, but too much friction for a broad “everyone can use it” toy.
- Reaction GIF/button sites — adjacent interaction model, but usually lack fresh meme-specific scoring and challenge loops.
- Quiz/scoring sites — proven viral mechanic, but generic and often spammy.
- Know Your Meme / explainer articles — useful for context, but passive; they do not let users participate.

### Star / Demand Signal

- Roger purchased `67speed.com` and set an explicit “10 million visitors in one day” ambition.
- The `6-7` trend is visible across TikTok, Instagram Reels, basketball edits, schools, mainstream media explainers, and parent/teacher backlash.

- The domain supports direct social sharing: `67speed.com` can be spoken, typed, screenshotted, and linked without explanation.

### Real Problem

People want to participate in viral trends quickly, but most creation flows are too high-friction: find the sound, pick a template, edit a video, avoid copyright problems, and hope the result works.

`67speed` removes that friction. It gives anyone a meme-native result in under 10 seconds.

### V1 Buildability

A strong V1 can be built as a static-first site with tiny client-side interactions:

- No accounts.
- No database required for launch.
- No copyrighted audio or video.
- No user-upload storage required.
- Deterministic share-card generation.
- Edge-cached static assets.
- Simple analytics that can survive traffic spikes.
- Cloudflare Pages/Workers or equivalent edge hosting suitable for sudden viral traffic.

## V1 Scope

### Core Experience

- Homepage at `/` with a giant “Start the 67 Speed Test” action.
- 6.7-second interactive test using taps, timing, or silly prompts.
- Result score such as `67 Speed: 82/100`, `Dangerously 6-7`, `Certified Hallway Menace`, or `Too Normal`.
- Shareable result card with unique copy and OG image.
- “Challenge a friend” link that preserves the result seed.
- Replay button with new prompt variants.
- Lightweight explainer: “What is 6-7?” without over-defining the meme.

### Game Modes

Start with one polished mode and keep the others as fast-follow variants.

- `Speed Test` — tap when the screen hits `6` then `7`; score timing and chaos.
- `Vibe Check` — answer 6 absurd prompts; get a 7-part diagnosis.
- `Is It 6 or 7?` — choose between two nonsense options; output a shareable archetype.
- `Teacher Mode` — harmless printable/classroom-safe explanation and mute-the-chaos joke mode.
- `Parent Mode` — “Why is my kid yelling six seven?” explainer with a playful translator.

### Share Outputs

- Static share URL: `/r/<result-id>/`.
- OG image for each result.
- Copy-to-clipboard captions for TikTok, Instagram, X, Slack, WhatsApp, and iMessage.
- Downloadable image result.
- Optional SVG badge/card: “My 67 speed is 82/100”.

### Daily Viral Loop

- Daily challenge at `/daily/`.
- Daily global prompt, e.g. “React when the number 6 appears, but do nothing for 7.”
- Date-based deterministic seed so the experience changes daily without a backend.
- Leaderboard copy can be simulated locally in V1 or held for V2; avoid fake global stats.

### Trend / Explainer Pages

- `/what-is-6-7/` — concise explanation with caveats.
- `/parents/` — non-condescending parent explainer.
- `/teachers/` — classroom-safe explainer and “brainrot containment” joke resources.
- `/about/` — site purpose, safety, copyright stance.

Noindex low-value generated result pages unless they contain enough original content or are intended for social sharing only.

## Launch Strategy

The launch should be engineered around sudden traffic and share loops:

1. Ship a tiny polished V1 that can survive a spike.
2. Make the result card funny enough to screenshot.
3. Add “challenge a friend” at the end of every flow.
4. Seed with short posts that do not over-explain the meme.
5. Offer parent/teacher explainers so mainstream curiosity traffic has somewhere to land.
6. Add daily challenge variants to create repeat visits.

Traffic posture:

- Assume most visitors bounce quickly; optimize for instant comprehension.
- Assume a viral spike could be mostly mobile and social in-app browsers.
- Avoid server-rendered bottlenecks in the critical path.
- Host on edge/static infrastructure from day one.
- Keep analytics privacy-friendly and resilient.

## 10 Million Visitor Design Notes

If Roger’s target is 10 million visitors in one day, V1 must be built like a static viral toy, not a normal app.

Requirements:

- Static-first delivery for `/`, `/start/`, `/daily/`, and explainer pages.
- Edge-generated or precomputed OG/result cards with aggressive caching.
- No database dependency for the main test.
- No login wall, no email capture wall, no heavy tracking wall.
- JavaScript bundle small enough for bad mobile networks.
- Graceful degradation if analytics, card generation, or noncritical APIs fail.
- Queue or disable expensive features under load.
- Clear cache headers and CDN configuration.

A 10-million-visitor day is plausible only if the site is simple enough that the share loop matters more than infrastructure complexity.
